[h1]Pennsylvania Station, 1910[/h1]
The lost Pennsylvania Station in New York, as McKim, Mead & White built it in 1910 and as it stood until 1963, at true scale. Walk in from Seventh Avenue through the Doric colonnade, down the 225 ft shopping arcade with its bronze shopfronts and lamp standards, past the Cassatt statue and down the grand stair into the main waiting room: three groin-vaulted bays 150 ft high, coffered vaults, giant Corinthian columns under an attic storey, coffered cross arches between the bays, thermal windows with morning sun on the floor, and rows of oak benches in the end bays. West of the waiting room is the steel-and-glass train concourse: glass groin vaults on branching lattice columns, a clock over the travertine arcade, and stairs down to the tracks. Below it are six island platforms with hanging lamps, seven tracks running to the tunnel portals, and two trains of Tuscan red coaches behind green DD1 electrics. Outside, the station front runs 430 ft along Seventh Avenue: a Doric colonnade between corner pavilions, the raised centre with its clock group (two reclining figures either side of the clock) and stone eagles, and the 31st and 33rd Street fronts with their carriage drives and arched gables. Off the stair hall are the restaurant's dining room, with oak wainscot, coffered ceiling, chandeliers and laid tables, and the lunch room with its long marble counters.
